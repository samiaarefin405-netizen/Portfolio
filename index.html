Thesis & Abaqus Numerical Models

Thesis Title: INFLUENCE OF VOID GEOMETRY ON THERMAL CONDUCTIVITY AND FIRE INTEGRITY (EXPERIMENTAL AND NUMERICAL ANALYSIS)

Abstract: As global energy demand continues to rise, the construction industry faces growing pressure to adopt materials that improve energy efficiency and thermal safety. This thesis investigates the thermal performance of five hollow ceramic block configurations through a combined experimental and numerical approach using Abaqus finite element software. Material properties obtained from laboratory testing were applied to numerical models at both the unit cell and single-block scales. The simulations showed strong agreement with experimental results, as demonstrated by the BS9H's predicted thermal conductivity of 0.2961 W/m·K against a measured value of 0.296 W/m·K. Transient heat transfer simulations were subsequently conducted over a nine-hour period on full wall assemblies and their corresponding room models, with the BS1 wall configuration used for experimental validation. The results confirm that internal block geometry significantly governs temperature lag and heat flux behavior, while homogenization provides an efficient macro-scale approximation of thermal response. The study also developed a temperature-dependent material model to assess wall behavior under fire conditions using a standard fire curve, enabling evaluation of heat propagation under progressive heating. Together, these analyses establish a validated framework for predicting the thermal behavior of masonry wall systems, offering practical guidance for designing building envelopes that are both energy-efficient and resilient under extreme thermal conditions.
